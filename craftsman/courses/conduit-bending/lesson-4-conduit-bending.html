Conduit Bending

EMT hand bending for the field — measure right, bend once, and land on the box.

⏱ 60 minutes 🔧 Apprentice level 🧮 Calculator + YouTube videos

Why Conduit Bending Is Lesson 4

You've completed Safety First, DC Theory, and AC Theory. Now you put knowledge into practice — conduit bending is the hands-on skill that separates rough apprentices from craftsmen who can actually run pipe.

This lesson teaches the math behind the marks, then gives you a conduit bending calculator and YouTube video tutorials so you can watch, calculate, and practice on the same page.

Learning Objectives

  • Identify bender components and how take-up affects your marks
  • Calculate 90° stub-up mark distances using standard EMT deduct values
  • Layout offset bends using angle multipliers and shrink
  • Plan 3-point and 4-point saddles over common obstructions
  • Use the QIC bend calculator to verify marks before bending

Know Your Bender

Every hand bender has reference points you must know cold:

Mark Use
Arrow Align with your mark for offset bends and the center of saddle bends
Star Back of 90° bend — used for back-to-back 90° layouts
Toe / heel Supports the conduit during the bend; toe faces the direction of bend growth
Take-up Conduit consumed during a 90° bend — accounted for in the deduct value
Field rule: Always bend from the same orientation. Flip the bender wrong and your stub height changes. Consistency is the QIC standard.

90° Stub-Up

The most common bend. To hit a target stub height:

Mark distance = Stub height + Deduct

Standard EMT deduct values for hand benders:

Conduit Deduct Gain
1/2" EMT5"3"
3/4" EMT6"4"
1" EMT8"6"
1-1/4" EMT11"8"
1-1/2" EMT14"10"
2" EMT16"12"

Need a 12" stub in 3/4" EMT? Mark at 12 + 6 = 18 inches from the end, line up the arrow, bend to 90°.

Offset Bends

Offsets shift the conduit parallel to itself — around a joist, beside a beam, or into a box entry plane.

Distance between bends = Offset height × Multiplier

Shrink = Offset height × Shrink factor (per inch)

Angle Multiplier Shrink per inch of offset
10°6.01/16" (0.0625")
22.5°2.63/16" (0.1875")
30°2.01/4" (0.25")
45°1.4143/8" (0.375")
60°1.05/8" (0.625")

Shallow angles (10°–30°) are standard for long runs. Steeper angles shorten the span but stress the conduit more.

Saddle Bends

Saddles carry conduit over an obstruction — a pipe, duct, or beam.

3-Point Saddle

Best for obstacles up to roughly 6" (for 3/4" EMT). Center bend at 45°, side bends at 22.5°.

  • Center-to-side distance ≈ obstacle height × 2
  • Shrink ≈ obstacle height × 3/4

4-Point Saddle

For larger obstacles. Four bends at 30° / 60° / 60° / 30° distribute the rise more gradually.

  • Segment spacing ≈ obstacle height × 1.5
  • Shrink ≈ obstacle height × 1
